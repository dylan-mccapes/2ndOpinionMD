# M68 ICM — v2.0 Roadmap (Deferred Items)

**Purpose:** This document captures every concept, enhancement, and architectural addition that was evaluated during the M68 v1.0→v1.1 audit process and explicitly deferred to v2.0. Each item includes the original concept, its clinical relevance, why it was deferred, and what must be true before implementation begins.

**Source audits:** Fluid Dynamics Audit (March 2026), ChatGPT Lymphatic System Audit (March 2026)

---

## Deferred Item 1: Sedimentation Modeling

### Concept (Fluid Dynamics)
In fluid dynamics, particles settle to the bottom of a vessel over time, reducing effective volume. Unlike discrete displacement objects (bricks), sediment accumulates gradually and is hard to observe until the vessel is nearly full.

### Clinical Analogue
Subclinical, slow-accumulating burdens that don't register as discrete stressors but progressively reduce capacity: years of micronutrient depletion, chronic low-grade gut permeability, slow heavy metal accumulation (e.g., mercury from dental amalgams), cumulative sleep debt that doesn't fully resolve on weekends, progressive deconditioning from sedentary lifestyle. These aren't "bricks" (displacement) — they're sediment. They reduce effective vessel volume slowly and invisibly.

### Why It Matters
The difference between displacement and sedimentation is clinically significant for intervention design. Displacement (bricks) can be individually identified and addressed. Sedimentation requires *systemic clearing* — deep nutritional repletion, extended recovery periods, detoxification support, comprehensive gut restoration. A patient with high sedimentation looks like they have "unexplained" low capacity despite having few identifiable stressors.

### Why Deferred
Modeling sedimentation requires distinguishing it from displacement computationally, which adds a fourth dynamic to the three-valve model. The data sources needed to estimate sedimentation (long-term micronutrient trend data, gut function markers, heavy metal panels) are often unavailable in routine clinical data streams. M64 (FUDD) flags could feed sedimentation estimation, but the mapping from FUDD patterns to sedimentation load needs clinical validation.

### Prerequisites for v2.0 Implementation
- M64 FUDD operating with sufficient patient history (≥6 months of longitudinal FUDD data)
- M21 vault containing ≥12 months of ICI time-series for calibration
- Clinical validation study confirming that the sedimentation construct adds predictive value over displacement alone
- Population-level data establishing sedimentation accumulation rates for common subclinical burden categories

### Proposed Schema Addition
```
SedimentLoad {
  patient_id:           string
  estimated_load:       float (0.0–1.0; reduces effective ICmax)
  contributing_factors: SedimentFactor[]
  confidence:           enum { HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW }
  last_updated:         timestamp
  data_sources:         Reference[] (M64 flags, lab trends, vault history)
}

SedimentFactor {
  label:                string (e.g., "chronic micronutrient depletion")
  category:             enum { NUTRITIONAL | TOXIC | INFLAMMATORY | STRUCTURAL | METABOLIC }
  accumulation_rate:    float (estimated increase per month)
  clearance_feasibility: enum { HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW }
}
```

## Deferred Item 2: Circadian Parameterization

### Concept (Fluid Dynamics: Thermal Effects)
Fluid properties (viscosity, flow rate, density) change with temperature. In the ICM analogy, time-of-day and circadian phase dramatically affect all three valves.

### Clinical Analogue
Cortisol peaks in the morning (modifying both inflow sensitivity and outflow capacity). Inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) peak in the evening. Mast cell degranulation thresholds shift with circadian phase. Histamine levels follow a circadian pattern with nocturnal peaks. A stressor at 3pm when cortisol is low and inflammation is rising has a different ICI impact than the same stressor at 9am.

### Why It Matters
Circadian modulation explains several clinical puzzles: why patients flare at specific times of day, why evening symptom reports are more severe than morning reports for the same objective load, why the same intervention has different efficacy at different times. Incorporating circadian phase into the ICI computation would improve prediction accuracy and enable time-specific intervention recommendations ("do your breathwork in the evening when your inflammatory cytokines peak").

### Why Deferred
Full circadian modeling requires: continuous wearable data (to infer circadian phase, not just clock time), cortisol diurnal patterns (or validated proxies), and sufficient patient history to calibrate individual circadian modifiers. The computational complexity is significant — each of the three valves would need time-varying coefficients. For v1.0/v1.1, the recency weighting on inflow partially captures temporal sensitivity.

### Prerequisites for v2.0 Implementation
- Wearable integration providing continuous circadian proxies (skin temperature, HRV patterns, activity timing)
- Literature-derived default circadian modifier curves for inflow sensitivity, outflow capacity, and displacement reactivity
- M21 vault containing time-stamped ICI data with sufficient granularity (≥4 computations per day) for circadian pattern extraction
- Clinical validation that circadian adjustment improves overflow prediction accuracy vs. time-agnostic model

### Proposed Architecture
Add circadian_phase (estimated from wearable + clock time) as a modifier on all three valve computations. Each valve gets a circadian_modifier curve (0.8–1.2 range) that scales its effective contribution based on time of day. Curves are initialized from population defaults and personalized from M21 data.

## Deferred Item 3: Cavitation / Let-Down Flare Detection

### Concept (Fluid Dynamics: Cavitation)
When pressure drops rapidly in a fluid, dissolved gases form bubbles that then collapse violently, causing damage to surfaces. The damage comes from the rapid pressure *change*, not from steady-state high or low pressure.

### Clinical Analogue
Rapid stress removal can paradoxically trigger a flare. The "let-down effect" — getting sick on the first day of vacation, crashing after a deadline, flaring after a relationship conflict resolves. The rapid change in allostatic load itself is destabilizing, independent of the absolute load level. This is well-documented in the immunology literature: cortisol suppresses immune function during acute stress, and rapid cortisol decline unmasks the suppressed immune response, producing inflammatory rebound.

### Why It Matters
Without cavitation detection, the system would interpret rapid ICI improvement (stress dropping) as purely positive. In reality, patients at high load who experience rapid stress reduction are at elevated overflow risk during the transition period. This creates a counterintuitive alert: "Your capacity is improving rapidly. This is generally positive, but rapid changes can temporarily destabilize your system. Continue your recovery practices and monitor for any unusual symptoms over the next 24–48 hours."

### Why Deferred
Modeling cavitation requires tracking the *rate of change* of ICI (dICI/dt), not just the absolute level. When dICI/dt exceeds a positive threshold (ICI is improving rapidly), the system would need to temporarily increase overflow probability rather than decrease it — which is architecturally counterintuitive and requires careful governance to avoid confusing patients or clinicians. Additionally, M13's trajectory volatility detection partially covers this from the instability side.

### Prerequisites for v2.0 Implementation
- ICI computation frequency sufficient to detect rapid changes (≥2x daily minimum)
- M21 vault data containing confirmed let-down flare events (retrospective identification)
- Clinical validation that dICI/dt-based prediction adds value over M13's existing volatility detection
- Patient communication templates that explain the counterintuitive "improving but temporarily vulnerable" state

### Proposed Detection Logic
```
if dICI_dt > cavitation_threshold AND ici_previous_24h < 40%:
    cavitation_risk = TRUE
    overflow_probability_modifier = +0.15  # temporary elevation
    patient_advisory = "let_down_caution"
    decay_window = 48h  # risk modifier decays over this period
```

## Deferred Item 4: Bernoulli Constriction Effects

### Concept (Fluid Dynamics: Bernoulli's Principle)
Fluid accelerates when moving through a constriction, and pressure drops in the constricted region. In the ICM analogy, this represents the effect of losing a key coping outlet.

### Clinical Analogue
When a patient's coping repertoire narrows (loss of exercise due to injury, loss of a social support relationship, loss of access to a meditation practice during travel), the remaining stressors flow through fewer channels. Each remaining stressor hits harder, not because the stressor itself changed, but because the processing channel has constricted. Loss of a single key coping mechanism can produce a disproportionate ICI drop.

### Why Deferred
This is real but overlaps significantly with the turbulence concept implemented in v1.1. If turbulence is working correctly (system-level amplification under load), the Bernoulli effect is partially subsumed — the non-linearity is captured by the turbulence coefficient increasing as ICI drops. Distinct constriction modeling would require tracking the *number of active outflow channels* (not just the aggregate outflow rate) and modeling per-channel flow acceleration, which adds significant complexity for marginal predictive gain over turbulence alone.

### Prerequisites for v2.0 Implementation
- v1.1 turbulence regime operating for ≥6 months with calibration data
- Analysis showing cases where turbulence alone underestimates ICI drop following outflow channel loss
- Outflow factor tracking with sufficient granularity to count active vs. inactive channels (not just aggregate level)

## Deferred Item 5: Fascial System Modeling

### Concept
Fascial restriction affects lymphatic flow, proprioceptive signaling, and mechanical tissue compliance. The fascia is the connective tissue matrix through which lymph vessels and nerves traverse; restriction in the fascia physically impedes lymphatic drainage and can create localized inflammatory pockets.

### Clinical Analogue
Patients with chronic pain, EDS/hypermobility, post-surgical adhesions, or prolonged immobility often have fascial restriction patterns that create localized lymphatic stagnation. Myofascial release, massage, and movement therapies that address fascial restriction can produce disproportionate symptom improvement — not through "releasing toxins" (a common misconception) but through restoring mechanical lymphatic drainage in restricted tissue regions.

### Why Deferred
Fascial restriction is difficult to quantify from available data streams (wearables, PROs, labs). It requires either: (a) clinician-reported fascial assessment, (b) specialized imaging (MRE, ultrasound elastography), or (c) proxy estimation from movement pattern data (which is not yet standardized). The evidence base for fascia-inflammation interaction, while growing, is less mature than for the other infrastructure variables (lymphatic tone, vagal tone).

### Relevance to M68
If implemented, fascial status would be a sub-component of `lymphatic_tone` (fascial restriction reduces lymphatic drainage efficiency) and would feed into the backpressure computation. Myofascial release therapies would be M66 wellness action candidates with measurable ICI impact potential.

### Prerequisites for v2.0 Implementation
- Clinical partnership providing fascial assessment data
- Validated proxy metrics for fascial restriction from available data streams
- M66 wellness action catalog including myofascial release / bodywork category
- At least one pilot study correlating fascial intervention with ICI improvement

## Deferred Item 6: Microbiome as Infrastructure Variable

### Concept
The gut microbiome modulates inflammatory signaling, nutrient absorption, neurotransmitter production, and immune function. Dysbiosis is both a displacement stressor (chronic inflammatory burden) and an outflow impediment (impaired clearance, impaired nutrient-based recovery).

### Clinical Analogue
Patients with gut dysbiosis have: impaired absorption of anti-inflammatory nutrients (affecting outflow via nutrition channel), elevated baseline inflammatory cytokines (adding to displacement), increased gut permeability permitting endotoxin translocation (adding to inflow), and impaired clearance of histamine and other mediators (increasing backpressure). The microbiome is arguably the single most cross-cutting infrastructure variable — it touches all three valves simultaneously.

### Why Deferred
The microbiome is extraordinarily complex and individual-specific. Current standardized assessment methods (16S rRNA sequencing, shotgun metagenomics) are not real-time, not widely available in clinical data streams, and lack validated thresholds for "healthy" vs. "dysbiotic" states. Proxy estimation from symptoms and dietary patterns is possible but low-confidence. M64 (FUDD) already captures some microbiome-related effects via FUD-GB (Gut-Barrier Dysfunction) mechanism.

### Relevance to M68
If implemented, `microbiome_status` would be an infrastructure variable alongside `lymphatic_tone` and `vagal_tone`, modifying outflow effectiveness (impaired clearance), inflow rate (endotoxin translocation), and displacement (chronic inflammatory burden from dysbiosis). It would be the most complex infrastructure variable to estimate and calibrate.

### Prerequisites for v2.0 Implementation
- Validated proxy metrics for microbiome status from available data streams (symptoms, dietary patterns, M64 FUD-GB flags, stool test results where available)
- M64 FUD-GB operating with sufficient longitudinal data to estimate gut barrier status
- Literature-derived default microbiome impact curves for each valve
- Clinical validation that adding microbiome estimation improves ICI prediction vs. the three-infrastructure-variable model (lymphatic_tone + vagal_tone + system_viscosity)
